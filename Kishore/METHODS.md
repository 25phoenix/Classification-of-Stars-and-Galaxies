### Dataset and identity

Galaxy10 DECaLS contains ten broad morphology labels derived from Galaxy Zoo votes and rendered DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys cutouts. The public DECaLS release documents 17,736 RGB images of 256 × 256 pixels. The HDF5 file is verified by SHA-256 before extraction. Original row order supplies the stable row IDs. Class names come from the DECaLS documentation. [Dataset repository](https://github.com/henrysky/Galaxy10) and [astroNN dataset documentation](https://astronn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/galaxy10.html).

Exact duplicates match on decoded RGB array bytes using SHA-256. Candidate repeated objects match when their valid RA/Dec positions are separated by at most 1 arcsecond on the sphere. The connected components of either relation define groups, with the smallest row ID as the group ID. The documented schema provides coordinates with no persistent object ID. This heuristic can combine nearby distinct sources and miss identities whose coordinates disagree by more than 1 arcsecond. Repeated rows, including any conflicting labels, retain their catalog labels and stay in one partition.

### Preprocessing and quality policy

Integer inputs in [0, 255] are divided by 255. Floating inputs must already lie in [0, 1]. Inputs must be finite, at least 16 pixels on each side, and grayscale or three-channel RGB. Rendered RGB intensity is the weighted channel sum with coefficients 0.2126, 0.7152, and 0.0722, with no linearization. The longer side is resampled to 128 pixels, preserving aspect ratio up to rounding. Downsampling uses Gaussian prefilter width (inverse scale − 1)/2 original pixels, then bilinear interpolation. Upsampling uses bilinear interpolation alone.

The sky is the border median, using a border 12.5% of the shorter side rounded to the nearest pixel. Noise is 1.4826 times the border median absolute deviation, floored at 1/255. Detection uses a 1-pixel Gaussian on the sky-subtracted image. It thresholds above 2.5 noise units and labels 8-connected components. Of components reaching within 18% of the shorter side from the geometric center, it selects the one with the nearest pixel. The center is its positive sky-subtracted flux centroid. The aperture is a circle of radius 2.5 times the equivalent circular segment radius, with a minimum of 4 pixels. Other detected components are masked. Sources blended into the target component can remain. The measurement population is the in-frame circular aperture minus these neighbors. Negative sky-subtracted pixels are clipped to zero. Outside this population the measurement image is zero.

The predeclared validity policy retains every row with seven finite values, including rows flagged for low signal or truncation. Low signal indicates a failed detection or average positive target flux below three noise units. Truncation indicates the aperture extends past a boundary pixel center. Uniform input, malformed input, failed detection, target components with fewer than five pixels, and undefined feature calculations yield missing values and exclude the row. Inference applies the same finite-feature rule and abstains on excluded inputs. The feature table includes all rows, flags and reasons. The run uses no imputation.

### Seven morphology measurements

1. **Concentration.** Compute cumulative positive flux of the measurement population by increasing radius around the detection centroid. Use linear interpolation from the first radius associated with each distinct cumulative flux to get r20, r50, and r80, relative to the total in-aperture flux. Compute 5 log10(r80/r20).
2. **Asymmetry.** Rotate the zero-masked measurement image 180° about the detection centroid with bilinear interpolation and zero beyond the frame. Sum absolute residuals over the image and divide by total in-aperture flux. The center is fixed and there is no residual sky-asymmetry correction.
3. **Smoothness.** Gaussian-filter the zero-masked measurement image with sigma max(1 pixel, 0.25 r50). Sum only positive original-minus-smoothed residuals within the measurement population at radii greater than 2 sigma. Divide by all in-aperture flux. No background residual is estimated.
4. **Entropy.** Form a 32-bin equal-width histogram over [0, 1] using measurement-population pixel intensities divided by their maximum, including zeros. Compute −Σ p log2(p) using nonempty bin fractions p.
5. **Gini.** Sort all n measurement-population fluxes ascending, including clipped zeros. Compute Σ(2i−n−1)fᵢ divided by (n−1)Σfᵢ with 1-based ranks.
6. **M20.** Use the positive aperture flux centroid, which minimizes total second moment Mtot = Σ fᵢrᵢ². Take pixels by descending flux, breaking ties in raster order, including the entire pixel that first reaches 20% cumulative flux. Compute log10 of their second-moment sum divided by Mtot. Zero moments are unresolved.
7. **Ellipticity.** Compute the aperture flux-weighted 2×2 spatial covariance about the aperture flux centroid. With eigenvalues λmin and λmax, b/a = √(λmin/λmax) and e = 1−b/a.

The CAS concepts are based on Conselice and the moment and Gini concepts on Lotz, Primack and Madau. These measurements are morphology-inspired proxies. Color rendering, geometric segmentation, clipping, finite resolution, the lack of a Petrosian aperture, and the fixed asymmetry center and lack of background residual corrections affect comparability with calibrated photometry and publication-grade CAS or statmorph. JPEG compression, sampling, and different surveys can shift the features for local cutouts. Scalar features can lose the arm structure, bar morphology, and other visual distinctions used by volunteers. Random Forest probabilities are raw model outputs and have no calibration guarantee. [Conselice 2003](https://doi.org/10.1086/375001) and [Lotz, Primack & Madau 2004](https://doi.org/10.1086/421849).

### Split protocol and training

Use seed 42 by default. Generate five candidate stratified partitions and reserve the fold nearest to 20% by the sum of absolute size-fraction error and L1 distance from the overall class proportions. Use StratifiedGroupKFold when groups repeat and StratifiedKFold otherwise. Every split is checked for exhaustive assignment, disjoint groups and all ten classes. Five outer folds use seed + 1. The three inner folds within each outer training partition use seed + 10 + the outer fold number. Final tuning on development uses five folds with seed + 2. The held-out examples never enter a search or refit.

The forest search has eight candidates. Each uses 160 trees and sqrt(7) rounded down as the feature-count rule. The grid varies maximum depth between 14 and unlimited, minimum leaf size between 2 and 8, and class weights between uniform and balanced subsample. GridSearchCV selects via macro-F1 with all ten labels. Search parallelism is across candidate fits and each forest is single-threaded. The majority baseline is fitted on each outer training partition and on all development rows for the final test. The serialized pipeline is the final development fit. Pooled OOF scores summarize concatenated predictions, while the outer mean gives each fold equal weight. Feature plots include all usable examples and are produced after evaluation.

Reproduce from Kishore with the setup commands in README.md, then run `.venv/bin/python main.py all` and `.venv/bin/python main.py check`. The run records package versions, split manifests, actual fit parameters, search tables, times, and per-row outputs. [Official data release](https://zenodo.org/records/10845026).
